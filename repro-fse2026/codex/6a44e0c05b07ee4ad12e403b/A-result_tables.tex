\section{Detailed Result Tables}
\label{app:detailed-results}

The main text reports the conclusions as RQ-oriented dashboards. This appendix
keeps the detailed numerical tables for auditability. Read the tables from left
to right as follows: RQ1 metrics (CV and $p_{90}/p_{10}$) diagnose cost spread;
RQ2 metrics ($\rho$ and $\Ratio$) diagnose budget calibration; RQ3 metrics
(top-$1$ flips and agreement) diagnose whether the recommended
strategy--budget combination changes;
and RQ4 metrics (frontier size and Jaccard) diagnose whether the set of
reasonable trade-offs changes.

\Cref{tab:app-test-evidence} records which hypotheses have classical
$p$-values and which are deterministic decision comparisons. In all cases the
main interpretation uses the accompanying effect size because the practical
question is not only whether an effect exists, but whether it changes a budget,
ranking, or feasible choice set.

H1b & RQ1 & Kruskal--Wallis tests of $\TrainCost$ across strategies at fixed

Maximum $\eta^2$ across levels. Small effects begin at $\eta^2\ge0.01$;

H2a & RQ2 & Spearman rank-correlation test between $n$ and $\TrainCost$;
report $\rho$ and $p$. &
Spearman $\rho$. High positive $\rho$ means sample size orders measured cost
well, even if the absolute budget can still be miscalibrated. \\
H2b & RQ2 & $95\%$ bootstrap confidence interval for the median $\Ratio$;
reported as interval evidence rather than a single $p$-value. &
$|\widetilde{\Ratio}-1|$. Values above $0.05$ mean the median sample spends at
least $5\%$ more or less measured budget than implied by sample count. \\
H2c & RQ2 & Kruskal--Wallis test of $\Ratio$ across strategies; report $p$. &
$\eta^2$. This separates global calibration from strategy-specific budget
bias. \\
H3a & RQ3 & Decision comparison across the cost-weight sweep; no population
$p$-value. &
Top-$1$ flip count or fraction, plus agreement/Kendall $\tau$. Any flip means
the recommended strategy--budget combination changes; the fraction gives severity. \\
H3b & RQ3 & Spearman test when enough systems or contexts are available. &
Correlation between cost spread and ranking instability. Positive values mean
heterogeneous costs tend to produce less stable rankings. \\
H4a/H4b & RQ4 & Pareto-front set comparison; no population $p$-value. &
Front-only combination counts and Jaccard overlap. Jaccard $=1$ means identical
fronts; $1-$Jaccard is the changed-frontier share. \\
H5a--H5c & RQ5 & Context tests use Kruskal--Wallis or Spearman evidence when
the context design supports it; otherwise they aggregate decision effects. &
H5a reports $\eta^2$ and ratio spread; H5b/H5c use flip-rate differences,
Kendall $\tau$, and Jaccard spread to measure how much the RQ2--RQ4
conclusions move across inputs, workloads, targets, releases, or datasets. \\
\bottomrule

Family & RQ1: cost spread & RQ2: budget agreement & RQ3: ranking impact & RQ4: Pareto impact & RQ5: context \\
\midrule
\StudyReplicationICSE &
Seven non-x264 systems span CV $0.072$--$1.981$; $p_{90}/p_{10}$ reaches
$47.9$ on BerkeleyDBC and $47.0$ on lrzip. &
$n$ is strongly correlated with measured cost ($\rho=0.879$--$0.943$), but
median $\Ratio$ ranges $0.797$--$1.056$. &
Stable on LLVM, Polly, Dune, HIPAcc ($0/7$ flips); unstable on lrzip ($5/7$
flips). CV predicts flips across these seven systems ($\rho=0.90$). &
Frontiers change on $5/7$ systems; Jaccard ranges $0.33$--$1.00$
(\Cref{tab:app-replication-systems}). &
Three regimes: validated, warning, changed assessment.\\
\midrule
\StudyReplicationICPE &
Time-cost CV is moderate across $17$ inputs ($0.21$--$0.39$); size-cost is
usually lower. &
Aggregate ratios are close to $1$, but strategy bias remains: Henard tends to
be cheap, $t$-wise costlier. &
Top-$1$ flips range from $6/119$ in time/time to $21/119$ when predicting size
while paying time. &
The asymmetric time-cost/size-target case has the lowest top-$1$ agreement
($0.824$) and low median Pareto Jaccard ($0.375$). &
Random and distance-based choices remain strong in some inputs, but the
robust-baseline conclusion is not invariant to measured cost.\\
\midrule
\StudyEnergy &
CV spans $0.058$ (PostgreSQL energy) to $7.104$ (jump3r performance), the
widest spread in the paper. &
$n$ remains correlated ($\rho=0.710$--$0.996$), but median $\Ratio$ spans
$0.168$--$1.035$. &
Top-$1$ rankings differ in $24/28$ cost--target scenarios; brotli flips at
every cost weight in three scenarios. &
Count and measured-cost Pareto fronts differ in all $28/28$ scenarios. &
Target/cost asymmetry repeats outside x264 and outside runtime cost.\\
\midrule
\StudyWorkload &
Across $93$ workload contexts, CV ranges $0.026$--$2.367$; within one system,
workload can move CV by an order of magnitude. &
Rank correlation is the most robust result (pooled support $0.95$), while
budget agreement is workload-sensitive (median $\Ratio$ $0.661$--$1.059$). &
Rank divergence has pooled support $0.80$; workload explains about $55\%$ of
within-system variance in top-$1$ agreement. &
Pareto-front change has pooled support $0.81$; $71/93$ contexts have Jaccard
$<1$. &
The same system can move between safe and unsafe regimes across workloads.\\
\midrule
\StudyLCBench &
Across $35$ non-pure software-system datasets, CV ranges $0.595$--$2.247$;
the worst case is MiniBooNE. &
$n$ stays positively correlated with runtime cost ($\rho=0.543$--$0.918$),
but median $\Ratio$ spans $0.669$--$1.012$. &
Top strategy--budget combinations change on $33/35$ datasets; median agreement is
$0.286$ and two datasets flip at all $7$ cost weights. &
Frontiers differ on $32/35$ datasets; Jaccard ranges $0.182$--$1.00$. &
Cross-dataset context sensitivity is strong: H5a supports with
$p=4.49\times 10^{-71}$.\\
\midrule
\StudyLinux &
Seven releases have storage-cost CV $0.99$--$1.43$ and $p_{90}/p_{10}$
$4.02$--$6.04$. &
Random, balanced, and coverage have near-$1$ budget ratios; diversity costs
$1.24$--$1.52\times$ its count budget at evaluated small-budget levels;
uncertainty is often cheaper but $n$-dependent. &
Top-$1$ flips range $4/7$--$6/7$ depending on release and level grid. &
Measured-cost Pareto fronts are larger on every release; diversity is dominated
under both cost models. &
Strategy behavior replicates, but active learning changes direction with
sample size and release.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table*}
